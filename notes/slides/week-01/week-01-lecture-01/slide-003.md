# Slide 3

![Original slide 3](../../../public/generated/week-01-lecture-01/slide-003.png)

## Explanation

 A computer network is a system that interconnects computing devices so they can exchange data and communicate. The important distinction is that a network is not merely a collection of machines: it includes the connectivity and rules that let those machines cooperate to provide services.

## Walkthrough

The diagram shows hierarchy by geographic scope. A **LAN** (Local Area Network) connects devices in a local area, such as a home, lab, or office. Multiple LANs can be connected through a **MAN** (Metropolitan Area Network), covering a city or metropolitan region. A **WAN** (Wide Area Network) covers a large geographic area and can connect networks across provinces, countries, or continents. The Internet is described here as a **network of networks**, because it interconnects many independently operated networks rather than being one single LAN.

Each participating device is a **node**. The slide gives computers, routers, and switches as examples. Imagine sending a message from one campus LAN to another: the message starts at a computer node, crosses local and larger-scale network infrastructure, and reaches a destination node.

## Connections and exam relevance

This layered geographic view is a classification scheme, not a complete description of how a network operates. Later slides will focus on the components and protocols that make the communication possible. The definition also connects directly to the client/server interaction from Slide 2.
