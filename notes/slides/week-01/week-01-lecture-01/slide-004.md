## Explanation

This slide separates three related but distinct areas of networking. Keeping them separate helps us understand what is being built, what it does, and how information physically travels.

## Walkthrough

**Distributed systems** are applications made from multiple independent computers that cooperate toward a common goal. Examples include Internet services, cloud platforms, and social-media systems. Distribution can provide scalability, reliability, and performance beyond what one computer can provide.

**Computer networks** study how those computers are interconnected and how packets—the units into which data is carried—move across networks. **Communications** studies how information is represented as bits and carried using signals over a physical or wireless medium.

The relationship is: distributed applications use network services; networking mechanisms carry packets; communication mechanisms carry the bits that make up those packets.

## Connections and exam relevance

This three-part split connects the application-level client/server idea from Slide 2 to the lower-level mechanisms that make it possible. It also previews the course’s movement from applications, through networks, down to signals and bits. [week-01-lecture-01, p. 4]
