# Slide 2

![Original slide 2](../../../public/generated/week-01-lecture-01/slide-002.png)

## Explanation

The slide shifts from the lecture roadmap to the network’s purpose: allowing clients to access services hosted by a server across the Internet. The user-facing goal is not “moving bits” for its own sake; it is enabling useful applications such as information retrieval, online banking, shopping, music, and video.

## Walkthrough

The laptops and desktop on the left are **clients**: end systems that request a service. The machine on the right is a **server**: an end system that provides a service or resources. The cloud in the middle represents the network infrastructure between them; the question mark emphasizes that the internal mechanisms are the subject of the course. A typical interaction is: a client sends a request, the network carries it to the server, and the server sends a response back.

For example, when a browser loads a shopping page, the browser is running on the client, the store’s web service runs on a server, and the Internet carries requests and responses between them. The network must therefore provide connectivity, identify endpoints, move data, and cope with delays or failures.

## Connections and exam relevance

This client–server pattern is the motivation for studying network components and protocols later in the lecture. It also prepares us for Lab 1, where communication is observed through an echo client and server. [week-01-lecture-01, p. 2]
