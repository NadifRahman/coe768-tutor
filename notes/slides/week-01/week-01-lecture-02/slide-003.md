# Slide 3

![Original slide 3](../../../public/generated/week-01-lecture-02/slide-003.png)

## Explanation

Links can differ in how communication is allowed to flow between their two endpoints. The three modes are simplex, half duplex, and full duplex.

## Walkthrough

**Half duplex** means both endpoints can transmit, but not at the same time. The arrows in the upper diagram point in opposite directions at different times. The slide gives a Wi‑Fi access point (AP) and client as an example: they take turns using the shared wireless channel.

**Full duplex** means both endpoints can transmit at the same time. The lower arrows point both ways simultaneously; the slide uses a link between routers as an example.

**Simplex** means communication is one-way only: one endpoint transmits and the other only receives. A keyboard sending input to a monitor is the slide’s example.

## Connections and exam relevance

These modes describe link directionality, which is separate from network size or device role. Half-duplex sharing also relates to the collision problem from Lecture 1: devices may need coordination because they cannot transmit simultaneously. [week-01-lecture-02, p. 3]
