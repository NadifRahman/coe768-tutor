## Explanation

A **Wide Area Network**, or **WAN**, connects networks across large geographic distances, crossing metropolitan, regional, or national boundaries.

## Walkthrough

WANs connect LANs and other networks so users and computers in one location can communicate with those in another. Their main function is relaying data over long-distance infrastructure, typically using many routers; the slide also mentions Layer 3 switches and WAN technologies such as MPLS, ATM, and Frame Relay.

The Internet is an example of a WAN because it provides global connectivity. A useful mental model is: switches connect devices inside local networks, routers connect separate networks, and a WAN uses that interconnection across a broad area.

## Connections and exam relevance

This extends the LAN and MAN classifications from Lecture 1 and the switch/router distinction from Lecture 2. Geographic scope describes where the network reaches; routers and long-distance provider infrastructure help make that reach possible. [week-01-lecture-02, p. 12]
