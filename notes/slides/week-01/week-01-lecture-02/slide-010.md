# Slide 10

![Original slide 10](../../../public/generated/week-01-lecture-02/slide-010.png)

## Explanation

“Wired” means that devices are connected through a physical cable-based medium. The cable may use copper wire, twisted-pair copper, or optical fibre, and can connect devices to one another, to another network, or to the Internet.

## Walkthrough

The slide introduces wired LANs using Ethernet, identified here as IEEE 802.3. It distinguishes classic Ethernet from switch-based Ethernet. In **classic Ethernet**, several computers attach to one long shared cable, forming a **bus topology**. Each computer has an Ethernet adapter, or **NIC** (Network Interface Card), that provides the network connection and a unique 48-bit **MAC address** used to identify that interface on the local network.

Because the classic bus cable is shared, multiple computers may contend for the same medium, connecting this design to the collision and resource-allocation problem discussed earlier.

## Connections and exam relevance

This slide contrasts a physical wired medium with wireless free space and introduces the NIC and MAC address as essential host-side components. The classic bus is historically important for understanding why Ethernet needed collision-handling methods; later Ethernet designs commonly use switches. [week-01-lecture-02, p. 10]
