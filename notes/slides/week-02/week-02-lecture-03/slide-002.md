## Explanation

This slide introduces two channel concepts: bit rate and bandwidth. Bit rate is the number of bits actually sent from source to destination per second. Bandwidth is the maximum rate at which the medium can transfer bits. [week-02-lecture-03, p. 2]

## Walkthrough

If a link sends 80 million bits per second, its bit rate is 80 Mbps. If the link's capacity is 100 Mbps, then 100 Mbps is its bandwidth and 80 Mbps is the current bit rate. The metric prefixes are decimal here: 1 Kbit = 10³ bits, 1 Mbit = 10⁶ bits, and 1 Gbit = 10⁹ bits. ISPs commonly advertise capacities in Mbps or Gbps. [week-02-lecture-03, p. 2]

## Connections and exam relevance

Bandwidth is a capacity limit, not automatically the speed every application experiences. Actual bit rate can be lower because of congestion, protocol overhead, receiver limitations, signal quality, or other bottlenecks along the path. [week-02-lecture-03, p. 2]

## Check your understanding
