# Slide 17

![Original slide 17](../../../public/generated/week-02-lecture-02/slide-017.png)

## Explanation

Physical-layer devices operate with the network's physical media and signal representation. They transmit raw bits as electrical, optical, or radio signals rather than making decisions about logical packets. Examples on the slide include network interface cards (NICs), repeaters, and hubs. [week-02-lecture-02, p. 17]

## Walkthrough

An NIC—also called an Ethernet card or network adapter—connects a host to the network and handles the host's physical network interface. A repeater regenerates or amplifies a signal so it can travel farther. A hub is essentially a multiport physical repeater that repeats incoming signals to connected ports. These devices do not perform IP routing. [week-02-lecture-02, p. 17]

## Connections and exam relevance

This reinforces the difference between physical transmission and logical forwarding. A physical device moves or reproduces signal energy; a switch examines data-link information such as MAC addresses, and a router examines IP information. The slide's layer assignment is conceptual—modern NICs commonly implement both physical and data-link functions. [week-02-lecture-02, p. 17]
